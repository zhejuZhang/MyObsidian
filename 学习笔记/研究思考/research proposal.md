**Research Proposal: Redefining Urban Spatial Influence Areas: From Micro-Environmental Causality to Dynamic GNN-Based Spatial Interaction**

**1. Introduction & Motivation**

Traditional urban facility influence areas and accessibility analyses often rely heavily on "Euclidean distance" and "static" assumptions. However, in real-world urban environments, spatial interactions are inevitably restricted by physical barriers (e.g., road network topologies, building facades, rivers) and fluctuate with the rhythm of human mobility. This research aims to break the assumption of static and symmetrical distance buffers. By leveraging spatial big data and GeoAI, the study proposes a non-Euclidean, dynamic spatial coverage prediction model constrained by both real-world physical features and spatiotemporal human activities.

**2. Phase 1: Causal Mechanisms of Street View Environments on Micro-Movement Routes**

This foundational phase focuses on understanding the causal impact of the visual characteristics of the urban built environment on active travel (walking and cycling) route choices.

- **Data Sources:** Integration of multi-source spatial data, including Google Street View (GSV) imagery, OpenStreetMap (OSM) networks, and micro-trajectory data from platforms like Strava and bike-sharing systems.
    
- **Methodology:** Moving beyond traditional correlation analysis, this phase will introduce hybrid causal discovery algorithms (GFCI and LvLiNGAM) to identify the causal network structure between environmental variables and route choices. Additionally, Eigenvector Spatial Filtering (ESF) will be utilized to eliminate the interference of spatial autocorrelation.
    
- **Objective:** To quantify the "actual travel impedance" generated by micro-physical environments, providing empirical resistance parameters for the large-scale network model in the next phases.
    

**3. Phase 2: Reshaping Static Spatial Influence Areas via Graph Neural Networks (GNN)**

Building upon the micro-level impedance quantified in Phase 1, this phase aims to predict the asymmetric and irregular actual service boundaries of spatial nodes within the complex urban fabric.

- **Graph Construction:** The urban space will be abstracted into a Heterogeneous Graph. Absolute physical barriers (e.g., rivers, large building complexes) will be encoded as disconnected or ultra-high resistance edges. The road network capacity and the micro-environmental impedance (from Phase 1) will be assigned as edge weights.
    
- **GNN Modeling:** By utilizing Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) or Graph Attention Networks (GAT), the model will be trained to predict the true radiation polygons of urban facilities under the absolute constraints of real-world physical barriers, essentially replacing traditional GIS buffer analysis.
    

**4. Phase 3: Dynamic-Contextualized Service Area Delineation Incorporating Temporal and Human Mobility Dimensions**

While Phase 2 addresses physical barriers, Phase 3 breaks the "static" limitation by introducing the temporal dimension and human mobility, transforming the influence area into a dynamic entity.

- **Concept:** A facility's effective service area expands and contracts dynamically based on time of day (e.g., day vs. night, weekday vs. weekend), real-time traffic congestion, and crowd density.
    
- **Methodology:** Extending the GNN model to a Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Network (e.g., STGCN). Time-series human mobility data (such as mobile phone signaling or temporal POI popularity) and dynamic traffic impedance will be integrated as dynamic edge weights and node features.
    
- **Objective:** To delineate "dynamic-contextualized" service areas that "breathe" with urban rhythms. This offers a paradigm shift from static capacity planning to real-time, context-aware urban service distribution.
    

**5. Alignment with Your Research**

This proposed framework aligns closely with your extensive research in facility location optimization, GeoAI mapping, and particularly your foundational contributions to spatiotemporal modeling (such as GTWR). I believe that integrating causal inference with Spatio-Temporal GNNs can offer a novel methodological perspective to the Urban Big Data Centre's (UBDC) ongoing efforts in urban analytics.

我的研究兴趣主要集中在空间分析与 GeoAI 领域。在拜读了您在设施选址空间优化、时空建模以及机器学习在城市环境应用方面的高深造诣与代表性成果后，我深受启发。我构思了一个分为三个阶段的研究框架，从微观环境因果推断走向中观空间相互作用预测，并最终落脚于动态时空建模，旨在彻底突破复杂城市环境中传统欧氏距离与静态缓冲区的局限性。

以下是我的研究计划大纲，恳请您审阅。如果能得到您的任何反馈与指导，我将不胜感激。

**研究计划：重塑城市空间影响范围：从微观环境因果推断到基于动态时空图神经网络的空间交互预测**

**1. 研究背景与动机**

传统的城市设施影响范围及可达性分析，往往高度依赖“欧氏距离”以及“静态”假设。然而，在真实的城市环境中，空间相互作用不仅受到路网拓扑、建筑物立面、水系等真实物理屏障（Physical Barriers）的切割与限制，更会随着人类活动（Human Mobility）的节律而产生时空波动。本研究旨在打破静态、对称的距离假设，利用时空大数据与 GeoAI，提出一种受真实地貌要素约束且具有时间动态特性的非欧几何空间覆盖范围预测模型。

**2. 阶段一：街景环境与微观运动路线的因果机制研究**

这一基础阶段聚焦于探究城市建成环境视觉特征对主动出行（步行、骑行）路径选择的因果影响。

- **数据源：** 整合多源空间数据，包括 Google Street View 街景特征、OpenStreetMap 路网拓扑，以及 Strava/共享单车的微观轨迹数据。
    
- **研究方法：** 摒弃传统相关性分析，引入混合因果发现算法（GFCI 与 LvLiNGAM）识别环境变量与路径选择的因果网络结构。同时结合特征向量空间滤波（ESF）技术，消除空间自相关对因果推断的干扰。
    
- **阶段目标：** 量化微观物理环境构成的“真实出行阻抗”，为后续的大尺度图网络模型提供基于实证的阻力参数。
    

**3. 阶段二：突破欧氏距离——基于图神经网络 (GNN) 的静态物理边界重塑**

基于阶段一量化的微观阻力，本阶段旨在预测复杂城市肌理下，空间节点受物理条件约束的非对称、不规则辐射边界。

- **图数据建构：** 将城市空间抽象为异质图（Heterogeneous Graph）。河流、大型建筑等绝对物理切割要素被编码为断连边或极高阻力边；路网通行能力与微观环境阻抗（阶段一产出）作为边权重。
    
- **GNN 模型训练：** 利用图卷积网络（GCN）或图注意力网络（GAT），训练模型以预测在真实地物屏障绝对约束下的设施真实辐射多边形，以此取代传统的 GIS 缓冲区分析。
    

**4. 阶段三：引入时间与人类活动维度的“动态-情境化”服务区划定**

在阶段二解决“物理绝对性”的基础上，阶段三旨在打破“静态”局限，引入时间维度与人类行为，使影响范围模型具备动态的“呼吸感”。

- **核心理念：** 设施的实际影响范围并不是一成不变的，而是随着昼夜交替、工作日/周末差异、实时路网拥堵状况以及人群密度等情境变化而动态收缩或扩张。
    
- **研究方法：** 将 GNN 模型升级为时空图神经网络（如 STGCN）。引入时序人流密度（如手机信令数据、POI 动态热度）与动态路网阻抗，将其作为动态边权重与节点特征输入模型。
    
- **阶段目标：** 构建随城市节律变化的“动态-情境化”服务区模型，实现从静态容量规划向实时、感知情境的城市服务配置范式转变。
