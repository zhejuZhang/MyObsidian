**Introduction:**

As AI technology continues to advance, its applications have expanded into numerous fields, reshaping modern research. Particularly in Geographic Information Science (GIS), AI are fundamentally transforming the method of the urban analysis. It relies on processing massive spatial datasets, making data security a critical skill for GIS students (Nastoska et al., 2025, p2). However, while students are becoming more proficient on using AI to process spatical information, there is an obvious gap in their understanding of data security and privacy protection (Nastoska et al., 2025, p11). Although students are expected to manage private geopolitical data such as coordinates and movement patterns, many lack the necessary training in privacy-preserving techniques (Chan & Hu, 2023, p14).  This essay discusses the need include the data security education in the GIS curriculum and evaluates strategies to address this educational challenge.

Firstly, the current disconnect between GIS students’ technical skill and their data security awareness will be examined,  before analysing possible solutions to integrate privacy education into future academic practices.

**Situation:**

Currently, AI-driven technologies are essential for processing complex spatial information within the urban analysis. Students frequently use AI to analyse urban patterns, such as traffic jam or population mobility, using real-time data form sensors and mobile devices. These analytical tasks require to collecte and process sensitive geographic coordinates and movement patterns. In professional GIS practice, this information is important for optimizing urban infrastructure and improving city planning (Nastoska et al., 2025, p6). Therefore, students are not only expected to master technical skill but also to manage these AI models. As GIS applications in “smart city” projects grow, students are significantly becoming both users and managers of these massive spatical datasets, making it necessary for them to have a deep understanding of how geographic information is collected and stored throughout the AI lifecycle (Nastoska et al. 2025, p2).
